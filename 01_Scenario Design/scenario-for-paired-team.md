## Fictional client organisation

**Name:** SilverTrust Talent
**Size:** ~60 employees, founded 2019
**What it does:** A recruiting agency that operates a dual model — outbound recruiting (approaching candidates) and inbound HR management (reviewing applications). Job openings from clients are published both on the company website (for organic Google search ranking) and syndicated to LinkedIn, Indeed, and other major job engines. The platform positions itself as making job search feel as simple as ordering an Uber and as personalised as Netflix recommendations.
**Location:** Berlin, Germany
**Tech stack summary:** ATS (Applicant Tracking System), LinkedIn Recruiter, Indeed Sponsored Jobs, basic CRM for client relationship management. No AI tooling currently in use.

## Stakeholder personas

> Note to paired team: hidden pain points are NOT included here. They will be revealed through the interview process.

### Persona 1 — Dilia Müller, Head of Talent Acquisition

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Role | Head of Talent Acquisition — oversees candidate matching, recruiter performance, and placement success metrics |
| Goals | Improve time-to-hire, maintain match quality, keep recruiters productive |
| Day-to-day | Reviews recruiter dashboards, handles escalations on hard-to-fill roles, meets with clients to align on candidate profiles |
| Relationship to AI | Sceptical. Believes relationships and recruiter expertise are irreplaceable. Will push back if AI feels like it is replacing human judgement. |

**Hidden pain point (for teacher only — do not share with paired team):**
Dilia's team is spending 80% of their sourcing time on manual admin — formatting candidate profiles, copy-pasting between the ATS and client emails, and re-screening candidates that were already rejected in a previous process. The matching logic in the current ATS is too blunt; recruiters compensate by doing everything manually. Dilia would never frame this as "we need AI" — she frames it as "my team is exhausted and I can't hire fast enough to compensate."

---

### Persona 2 — Oli Vargas, Recruiting Operations Manager

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Role | Recruiting Operations Manager — manages inbound application flow, ATS configuration, recruiter workload prioritisation, and pipeline health |
| Goals | Improve signal-to-noise ratio in the applicant pool, reduce screening time without losing fit quality, optimise time-to-hire |
| Day-to-day | Monitors application volume per role, triages recruiter queues, runs weekly pipeline health reviews, troubleshoots ATS bottlenecks |
| Relationship to AI | Cautiously open but data-driven. Has seen five platforms promise to fix recruiting. Wants to see numbers, not promises. Specifically worried that AI will increase volume without improving quality. |

**Hidden pain point (for teacher only — do not share with paired team):**
Oli is sitting on a backlog of over 2,000 unreviewed applications across 40 open roles. Recruiters are skipping the bottom 60% of the queue entirely because there is no time. AI-generated CVs have made it harder, not easier, to tell strong candidates from weak ones — the writing quality is now uniform but the underlying fit is not. Oli has considered turning off the "Apply Now" button on some roles to stop the bleeding, but cannot do so without client approval.

## Candidate use cases

1. Automated first-pass screening of inbound applications against a structured job spec — scoring for fit before a recruiter opens the CV
2. AI-assisted candidate profile summarisation — turn a raw CV into a structured one-page brief for the recruiter
3. Job description optimisation — analyse which JD formats attract higher-quality applicants and suggest improvements
4. Long-tail matching — surface candidates from the existing database who were previously rejected for a different role but fit a new opening

## Notes for role-playing

When the paired team interviews you, stay in character. Dodge direct pain-point questions 2–3 times before revealing. Reward good indirect questioning.

Tension to maintain across personas:
- Dilia wants quality and human judgement preserved — she will resist anything that feels like automation replacing expertise
- Oli wants volume control and efficiency — he is open to AI but only if it reduces noise, not adds to it
- The two personas have different definitions of "a good outcome": Dilia measures by placement quality, Oli measures by pipeline throughput
